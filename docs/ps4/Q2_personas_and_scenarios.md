# Q2: Personas and Scenarios

The personas summarize user goals, tasks, constraints, frustrations, and expectations to inform the scenarios. They are composite profiles based on the supplied interviews, not records of individual participants. The report uses participant codes and contains no identifying participant details.

## Resident

### Persona summary

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Name | Student resident |
| Role | Student resident, Student Village |
| Summary | Reports a fault when it happens and wants to know what is being done and how quickly it will be handled. |
| Background | Lives in a student residence. Reports faults by phone, by email, at the counter, or through the online form. Has no FIMS account. Uses the web application in a phone browser where access is needed. |
| Short-term goal | Get the fault fixed correctly, without having to call again. |
| Long-term goal | Know what to expect for any repair, including how quickly it will be handled, and trust that the follow-up will happen. |
| Typical tasks | Reports a fault in the room, or follows up on an existing report.<br>Waits for the work to be done, and checks whether it has been done.<br>Confirms, or reports again, when the work does not solve the problem.<br>Visits the office in person when a fault feels serious (R2). |
| Frustrations | Often does not have a ticket number, so staff must find the ticket by room (S1).<br>Emails the director rather than the call center, and often does not read system replies (OFAM-1, OFAM-2).<br>Work can be done while the resident is away, and may not solve the real problem (R1).<br>Serious faults reported in person are handled quickly, but residents may not know that this route exists (R2, interpretation). |
| Constraints | No FIMS account. Contacts the call center by phone, email, or at the counter (OFAM-1, OFAM-2).<br>May not be in the room when a Technician/Contractor visits (R1). |
| Expectations | A clear record of what was reported and what was done.<br>A way to follow up on the same report, without starting a new one (design expectation, based on R1 and S1).<br>A notice when work is delayed, with the reason and expected date (design expectation, based on OFAM-1, OFAM-2 and T1).<br>A clear way to report a serious fault that gets same-day attention (design expectation, based on R2 and OFAM-3).<br>An email notice with the ticket number, which the resident can keep for reference (observed in R2's account). |

This narrative is illustrative [R1]. It is built from [R1] and is not a record of any participant [R1].

R1 followed up by phone [R1].

## Call Center Staff

### Persona summary

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Name | Call Center Staff |
| Role | Call Center Staff |
| Summary | Takes calls and emails, issues tickets for callers, and routes jobs to units. |
| Background | Receives requests by phone, email, and at the counter. Issues a ticket using the caller's details. Routes the job to a unit. |
| Short-term goal | Find the right open ticket quickly when a resident calls. |
| Long-term goal | Reduce repeat calls and missed updates. |
| Typical tasks | Issues a ticket for a caller by phone (S1).<br>Looks up a ticket by room when the resident does not have the ticket number (S1).<br>Checks progress with the unit, and answers resident follow-ups (S1). |
| Frustrations | Has to reload the system to see replies. Updates come in batches, so some are missed and need extra follow-up (S1).<br>A reply sent by email is not attached to the ticket, so staff must open a new ticket or take a call (S1).<br>Room lookup is done by hand (S1). |
| Constraints | Works in the current FIMS interface, which refreshes at a set interval (S1).<br>A reply appears only if the ticket is open on screen (S1). |
| Expectations | New replies appear on the ticket in real time, without reloading (S1).<br>Room-based lookup of open tickets (S1). |

## Technician/Contractor

### Persona summary

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Name | Technician/Contractor |
| Role | Technician/Contractor |
| Summary | Receives jobs as ticket numbers from the call center, works them until finished, and arranges subcontractors for larger work. |
| Background | Works from a list of jobs in progress, not a job board (T1). Arranges quotations and purchase requests for larger work through subcontractors (T1, C1). |
| Short-term goal | Finish each job correctly and record its true status. |
| Long-term goal | Keep parts available and finish work on schedule. |
| Typical tasks | Receives the ticket number from the call center (T1).<br>Checks the store for parts. If stock is out, holds the job and tells the resident the expected wait (T1).<br>Completes repairs, often recording completion in batches (T1).<br>Arranges quotations and purchase requests for larger work (T1, C1). |
| Frustrations | Parts are often out of stock, and purchases wait for the quarterly budget (T1).<br>Urgent purchases need finance approval, which is slow (T1).<br>Performance measures are reviewed quarterly and given out manually, outside the system (C1). |
| Constraints | Works from jobs in progress, with no job board (T1).<br>Performance results are not in FIMS (C1).<br>Completion forms are signed on paper when the resident is present (OFAM-1, OFAM-2). |
| Expectations | A clear job record with the ticket number and location (design expectation).<br>A hold option with a reason and an expected date (supported by T1's practice).<br>A way to record completion when the resident is not present (design expectation, based on T1).<br>Access to their own results in the system (design expectation, based on C1). |

## Scope note

Account R2 describes a gas-pipe fault that the resident experienced as serious. OFAM confirmed that it recognised the seriousness of the fault, sent the available Technician/Contractor immediately, and that Call Center Staff created and assigned the ticket at the same time (OFAM-3). The project's current workflow allows Call Center Staff to move a new ticket directly to ASSIGNED, so no new workflow state is needed. The design must support a same-day path, and the report records this as a change to the workflow description.

## Limitations

The prototype session was informal.

The personas are composite profiles rather than accounts of individual participants.
